My Programming Journey To GSoC
All Paths Lead To One

published on Thu, May 18, 2023 | 10 minutes read

  • Posts
  • My Programming Journey To GSoC

This is kind of a story like post and you won’t find anything technical here. Please skip this post if you find it not interesting :-) Why am I writing this? I just cleared GSoC 2023 and I want to just log everything I remember about my programming journey.

Time Machine

All Paths Lead To One! The One You Are Following At The Moment!

So, from where shall I start? There are many events that lead to this path, and in fact I’d say all the events and paths I followed before lead here.

  • Me being an academically mediocre student my whole life, not much interested in getting marks. I can study but I’ve always had this ego of idea between understanding all concepts and mugging them up (rote learning). Eventually both are useful but it eees what eet eeeezzz. Due to this I always (and still am) weak in subjects like Social Studies (Civics, Geography, Political Sciences etc…), Environmental Studies. Well I shall say I’m weak in scoring marks, not weak in them! to be more precise.
  • My father purchasing a brand new desktop for studying when I was in IV grade (iirc, or maybe it was V)
  • Me spending lots of my time playing games, especially Microsoft Games Chess! There was a time when I was easily able to defeat level 3 AI. It was Windows 7 Ultimate Edition back then and I really loved the theme! That large dell monitor, ASRock motherboard with 2GiB RAM and 512GiB HDD storage left lots of truly invaluable memories in my heart <3
  • My love for games attracting me to the idea that I should make my own game!
  • Programming? What’s that? Oh, c’mon! I don’t even know to code
  • All computers read binary? Let’s write 010101010… in notepad and save the file with ".exe" extension! Will it run? Will it suddenly destroy my computer? What will happen?
  • HTML! I can make my own sites now! I used to create a web page that listed all my games on my pendrive, one that I used to trade for games XD. You click the link and the game will be downloaded into your machine. The website used to have a popular Assasins Creed III background imag, a navbar and some other static stuff. Plain HTML without any CSS (because I didn’t know it back then!)
  • A basic simple interest calculator in JAVA? Woah! Do I need to write this much just to do basic maths? Where can I learn more? Do I need to remember different program for different purpose?
  • C#? Let’s create a software for a billing system for a Cafe
  • Wow! I can create softwares in Visual Basic!! GUI! Wow! Yay! (I’m literally having goosebumps when I recall those times. I was maybe in VI or VII grade back then.)
  • That folder virus creation virus that I used to create using batch scripts and prank with my friends.

“Hey! you run this file and it’ll install the game on your PC!”

  • Computer Sold :-(

Story ends? Nope, obviously not! I believe one of the main reasons that held me back from gaining more experience in programming than any other kid in a much developed country was the fact that India didn’t have much good internet facilities as it has now! I was very curious to learn, I used to follow my computer teacher after school recess to teach me HTML. I would go around some seniors to ask for games and softwares. Softwares that I didn’t even need, just for looking cool XD. But unlike me, less than 5% kids in my school were into coding! I really love my father for pruchasing that computer, even though tbh that PC wasn’t purchased for me or for playing games at all XD, I was the one who used it the most! I completed NFS Most Wanted 2007 Black Edition 3 times and still never got bored. To try different new Operating Systems, I used to reinstall Windows a lot and in that process I got to know some things about Operating Systems the practical way!

Again, as I said internet was too costly and a rare thing back then. In around 300 INR, you’d get only 1GiB 3G data that’d get exhausted the moment you opened a site with lots of images or an advertisement video! We used to have a dongle. Ah, those good times… 99.99999999999….% of the time.

Fast forward almost 2 years after I sold my computer, I got my new laptop, one which I’m using to write this post atm. Almost everyone was against the idea except me and my parents. They were cautious but still the bought it for me :-) I was happy again and this time I had access to lots of internet. Lots and lots and lots … and I can keep going. This was time of India getting digitized. Jio Communications was at peak of selling cheapest and one of the fastest 4G networks back then. Using that I’d explore the internet world wildly. Getting to know what can I do with this computer? Came across computer vision. I actually wanted to learn how missile targeting systems work but just followed this tangent of computer vision which I found really interesting! and then I started making some youtube videos after learning them from a OpenCV cookbook. My OpenCV installation video really got popular during lockdown period (almost 2 years after I uploaded them). You can go through the full playlist (not much useful).

Then thanks to YouTube’s recommender system I started getting videos about AI and how we can use them to do interesting things. I was in XIth back then, I came across lot’s of interesting channels

  • Code Bullet’s Snake AI is really interesting
  • sentdex’s Python Plays GTA V was really interesting and I wanted to do similar thing but my laptop isn’t that powerful and I didn’t want to use Python, because I was really biasted towards C++ back then. Not because I didn’t new python, it was one of my subject in CBSE boards but because I find it really slow and waste of CPU resources. There’s lot to this story so please don’t be offended, if you want a debate, ping me at my email and I’ll reply back.

There were more and more but mostly these two enticed me and then came the time I really learned how to program things! I came across a video that explained (with code!) how to create a Neural Network in C++! I didn’t even know how separate header files and source files work in C++, how classes are created and used, what are references and all. I was just doing C in C++. This video introduced me to really interesting things in just one hour. Well, the video is just one hour but it took me one whole day to consume it! I still remember, next day was my Chemistry test, my teachers just wanted one chance to embarass me and I gave them one for the sake of “Creating My Own Neural Network!” and that to in C++. Why it’s a big deal? Well because back then there were resources only for python! I came across an ML Library named MLPACK but it had it’s own learning curve and was way too much that what I was asking for! Time passed by, I started gaining experience in programming and ML and I created a new model, a faster version of the old Neural Network with help of Eigen and I created a blog post too on it which I guess still comes first if you search for “neural network in C++”. It was for a contest during lockdown but I didn’t win because some dumb interns probably think that they are smarter than the author themselves and they keep changing your code thinking that they are improving it but they are not, because they are an idiot!

Partial knowledge is more dangerous that having no knowledge at all, because it gives you a false sense of superiority which you don’t have!

It’s something that my elder sister used to say when we had an argument :-)

Time kept passing by and I got involved more and more, so much so, that I started skipping my coaching classes and studying regularly. I have no regrets for that at the moment because I was working hard where I needed to work. I learned lots of other interesting things. I learned how to create 2D games using SFML. I came across OpenGL then and then Vulkan was recently released, just 2 years before I came across it! I gave it a try and was and still is hard for beginners. I really like Vulkan tho for it’s speed and low level design. After giving my entrace exams and failing in them, taking a drop and all, I started programming again after being away from it for about an year. I developed my own very basic Kernel (Operating System’s core in layman terms) and I tried lots of things! You can literally see my programming journey on my GitHub Profile. I learned how to create GUI applications using wxWidgets. There was a time where I used to struggle over selecting proper project name before starting it. What directory structure should I choose? How do professional developers do it? What is CMake? I started playing CTFs then out of nowhere after I came across this awesome reverse engineering discord channel and someone posted that after two days there’s going to be FlareOn 9 CTF. That was oficially my first CTF and I played really bad. Out of 9 or 12 levels, I cleared two on my own but got stuck in 3rd, and, everyone hated that challenge that year because it was too guess and almost no reverse engineering was involved!. I helped a senior CTF team member develop a GUI for his VM to create an R2Wars like game. We called it XWars.

Everything’s been intresting up until now! and I sure hope it will be in future. I’d say I’m lot more experienced now than I was 1 year before in lots of things. For example, now I don’t struggle over project names and I just pick them randomly XD. This sums up almost the non-technical part of my programming story. Next comes GSoC and I get selected into Rizin organization for RzIL MIPS Uplifting.

How? First of all, having access to a desktop at an early stage got me into programming. Getting interested in AI/ML got me started with basic programming in C++. I know it sounds strange but I didn’t learn programming by solving DSA questions! Getting into GUI programming helped me learn about build systems. Learning Vulkan and OpenGL? well, thats some side knowledge and is useful in reverse engineering in later stages! Learning Vulkan, I used to write about 1000+ lines just to start the engine, so that broke my 1000 line code limit and I started writing more and more lines of code easily. Then I learned about Operating Systems and tried to build one. How is it being used atm? I’m writing uplifters and I need to take reference from ISA references. These are details about how an instruction works, and how “Operating Systems” and normal programs must sometimes handle if something goes wrong! The list goes on.

I also mentioned that lack of internet was the reason I still know so less. India is now much digitized and I’ve learned so much because of this. I can proudly say that whatever programming I’ve learned, my school and college didn’t teach me. They might take credit for it, but no! I’ve met really good teachers in my life, but it’s usually either me knowing the things already and wasting my time in the class just for attendance or me thinking that I really don’t need this right now and I can learn it later. Mostly it’s the former case up until now when it comes to programming.

One more thing that happened during this digitization is that India got digitized too fast. It didn’t get a change to tighten up it’s security. I’d say it’s still weak when it comes to cybersecurity but I see lots and lots of people rushing towards this field just like I am. So I guess this internet story is not completely mine but of all those who ever wanted to do something but weren’t able to do it because of lack of resources.

All the paths you follow lead to one, the one you are following!

This sums it up and in the next post I’ll write all the technical details of what I have contributed in Rizin, what things I need to do during this summer and some other interesting stuffs. See you later :-)
